TASK 3 — PEDAGOGY & CONTENT DESIGN
===========================================================

AI PROMPT USED:
Check out this Task. (Attached screenshot). I'll be choosing Science as a topic and i'll be teaching kids
about seeds and what they need for the to grow. 

AI-GENERATED OUTPUT:
A lesson plan using two watered/sunlit vs. dry/dark seedlings as the opening
hands-on comparison, a learning objective for parents, a moment where the
teacher introduces the word "needs" only after the children describe the
difference themselves, a check-for-understanding question, one key
instruction given in both English and Kiswahili, and a written rationale
explaining the sequencing. The AI also flagged that the exact CBC
strand/sub-strand wording it used should be checked against the real
Curriculum Design document, since it wasn't fully certain of the precise
official terminology.

CHANGES I MADE:
I changed the opening experience from seedlings to seeds (one watered and in
sunlight, one dry and in the dark), changed the learning objective to also
mention what plants produce when they mature, reworded the "introducing the
key term" section to have the teacher congratulate the children before
introducing the word "needs," reworded the "check for understanding" question
and the English/Kiswahili instruction to refer to "seeds" instead of
"plants," and wrote the "Why i planned the lesson that way" section in my own words while keeping the
same four-part structure (comparison before vocabulary, child's own
description first, the question as a clarification tool, and the bilingual
instruction targeting the observation moment).

WHY:
I switched to seeds because I felt it gave a clearer before/after demonstration
for this age group and showing seeds rather than already grown seedlings makes
the link between "what a plant needs" and "what happens if it doesn't get
those needs" more concrete, since the difference in outcome is more visibly
tied to neglect over time. I added the "what plants produce when mature" line
to the objective so the lesson also touches on the idea that meeting a
plant's needs leads to a visible outcome (flowers/fruit), not just survival.
I reworded the rationale into my own words because the original phrasing,
while accurate, didn't fully match how I would explain my own reasoning to a
marker, and I wanted the written rationale to clearly be in my own voice.
